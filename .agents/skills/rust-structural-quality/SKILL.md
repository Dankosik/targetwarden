# Rust Structural Quality

**Deletion test.** Judge every structure by the responsibility it owns and the facts a reader or caller must coordinate. Before settling a non-trivial public seam, sketch a 3–8 line caller walkthrough (happy path plus worst failure or interruption) and deepen the interface when callers must coordinate multi-step ceremony; keep structure when it hides complexity or preserves an invariant, and collapse it when it merely relocates the same reasoning, as shown in [deep seam examples](references/deep-seams.md).

**Module boundaries.** Follow the [repository architecture](../../../docs/architecture.md) for the strictly acyclic Functional Core / Imperative Shell layout across the 17 modules in `src/`. Do not introduce extra crates or sub-module micro-trees unless a real lifecycle or build-time boundary requires them; split or extend modules by coherent responsibility or authority rather than line count, keep `lib.rs` clean, and preserve layer direction (`report` never depends on `discover`, `execute`, or `platform`).

**Polymorphism and seams.** Choose a concrete type for fixed behavior, an enum for closed alternatives, generics for required static variation, and `dyn` when consumers need type erasure or heterogeneous storage, weighing API, binary-size, and compile-time trade-offs. Separate pure state transitions from I/O (Functional Core accepting `now: u64` Unix epoch rather than injecting a `Clock` trait or virtual `FileSystem` mock), collapse single-implementation traits introduced solely for internal mocking (`trait Platform` is the single explicit OS boundary), follow one idiom per concern, and require macros to earn what functions, generics, or traits cannot express cleanly.

**Functions and receivers.** Push `if`s up and `for`s down: move precondition and mode branching to the owning caller so inner helpers run straight-line, and push batching or iteration down into leaf operations. Extract a single-caller private function when its signature expresses a complete decision without reading its body; keep trivial forwarding and local-state fragments inline. Use methods for meaningful receivers, plain functions for stateless one-shot operations, and private context structs where grouped state clarifies ownership, collapsing one-off `Drop`-closure or trait-adapter wrappers satisfied by `std` or declared crates; see [responsibility examples](references/responsibility.md). Treat broad names (`manager`, `service`, `helpers`) as prompts to inspect ownership rather than automatic defects.

**Co-change and single ownership.** Trace one realistic change: pieces that must change together through a copied rule, implicit call order, or split state belong under one owner or a compiler/test check. Place shared pure domain types in `src/model.rs` and pure policy in the matching Functional Core module (`pressure`, `safety`, `sweep`, `rules`). Exhaustive matches already surface new variants, independent similar text may stay separate, and flag-accumulating helpers warrant checking whether responsibilities diverged.

**Coupling and surface area.** Reduce coupling before adding layers by tightening visibility (`pub(crate)` by default), narrowing parameters or results, or moving decisions to the existing owner (`rules`, `safety`, `sweep`, `execute`, or `platform`). Keep CLI and JSON surfaces minimal and single-path. Retain shims or fallbacks only with an accepted requirement, owner, observable trigger, and removal condition, and verify where rollback or error-mapping guarantees live before collapsing a container or wrapper. Judge structure by ownership and behavior rather than line-count or syntax taste.
